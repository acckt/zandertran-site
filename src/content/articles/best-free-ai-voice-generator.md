## Three Tools You Won't Find Here, and Why

**Play.ht** is dead. It shut down permanently at the end of 2025 after Meta's acquisition. Any list still recommending it hasn't been updated in a while.

**LOVO** doesn't have an ongoing free plan, despite showing up in nearly every "best free AI voice tool" roundup. What it offers is a 14-day trial of its Pro plan, then a paywall. A trial isn't free. It's a sales funnel.

**WellSaid Labs** has the same issue: a 7-day trial, not a real free tier. It's a strong paid tool, covered in the [paid comparison](/articles/best-ai-voice-generator), but it doesn't belong on a genuinely-free list.

## 1. ElevenLabs

**Free Allowance:** 10,000 credits a month.
**The Catch:** limited compared to paid tiers, but the voice quality is the strongest of anything on this list, free or paid.
**Best For:** testing whether voice cloning and realistic narration are worth paying for before committing.

## 2. Murf

**Free Allowance:** 10 minutes of voice generation a month.
**The Catch:** no downloads, no commercial rights. You can hear the output, but you can't publish it without upgrading. This is a demo, not a usable free tier for real content.
**Best For:** deciding whether Murf's polished, business-ready voice style fits your project before paying for it.

## 3. Fliki

**Free Allowance:** roughly 3 credits a month, about five minutes of content.
**The Catch:** watermarked output, and a thin allowance even by free-tier standards.
**Best For:** a quick first look at Fliki's voice library and video-generation combo, not ongoing use.

## 4. TTSMaker

**Free Allowance:** twenty voices marked unlimited, with the rest capped at 20,000 characters a week.
**The Catch:** a smaller, lesser-known brand, so support and long-term reliability are less proven than the bigger names on this list.
**Best For:** simple, occasional text-to-speech needs where you don't need a specific branded voice.

## 5. Free.ai

**Free Allowance:** 30,000 tokens a day, and no signup required at all.
**The Catch:** newer and less established than the other tools here. Worth using for a quick one-off, less proven for anything you'd build a workflow around.
**Best For:** a genuinely zero-friction test, since there's no account to create before you hear the output.

## 6. NaturalReader

**Free Allowance:** 20 minutes a day of premium voice access.
**The Catch:** built more for reading existing text aloud (documents, articles) than for producing standalone voiceover content, similar to Speechify's positioning in the paid comparison.
**Best For:** listening to your own written content read back to you, useful for proofreading by ear more than for publishing narration.

## 7. Kveeky

**Free Allowance:** 30 minutes a month, across more than 400 voices.
**The Catch:** a smaller brand with less independent review coverage than the others here, so treat the voice-quality claims with a bit more caution until you've heard it yourself.
**Best For:** someone who wants a wide variety of voice options to sample without any cost.

## How to Actually Evaluate a "Free" Tool

Two questions cut through most of the marketing noise around free AI tools, voice generators included. Is the free tier ongoing, or a trial with an expiration date disguised as a plan? Two of the tools that usually show up on lists like this failed that question outright, which is exactly why they're not on this one.

The second question matters just as much and gets skipped more often: does the free tier let you actually publish anything, or does it only let you preview the output? Murf's free tier answers that one honestly against itself. Real quality, zero usable output without paying. A tool can be genuinely free to try and still be useless to actually ship with, and the two aren't the same claim even though marketing pages tend to blur them together.

Run any new AI tool you're considering through both questions before trusting a "free forever" claim. It takes five minutes and it would have kept LOVO and Play.ht off most of the lists that still recommend them.
